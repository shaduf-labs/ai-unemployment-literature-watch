Research check · 20 October 2026

A broad U.S. AI jobs tracker still finds no clear footprint

Yale Budget Lab’s 14 October update reports no clear AI-related shift in the U.S. occupational mix. Its AI-use measures show no connection to changes in employment or unemployment, and its exposure-based comparison does not clearly show an AI labor-market footprint. This does not rule out a loss in a particular job, firm or entry route.

What was checked

We revisited the same tracker first recorded in September, not a new independent study. The update summarizes occupational mix, AI-use measures and a synthetic difference-in-differences comparison based on AI exposure. These are broad labor-market checks, not a count of AI-attributed layoffs or a randomized test of one employer’s tool rollout.

What it cannot answer

The short October page does not show the full updated data vintage or detailed estimates, and the linked interactive was not text-readable in this check. We therefore do not attach a new numeric effect size or claim a later data cutoff. A broad result can remain flat while a specific graduate group, employer or region changes. The narrow Copilot hiring studies use different outcomes and do not become an economy-wide verdict either.

Bottom line

The latest visible tracker summary reinforces the need to separate a broad negative check from narrower warning signals. It does not change our current answer: measured AI-related hiring evidence remains mixed and limited, while the 6.1 million-worker vulnerability estimate describes conditional exposure and transition capacity, not lost jobs.
