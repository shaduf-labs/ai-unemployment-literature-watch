Evidence update · 17 September 2026

Texas shows an AI-related hiring pullback. The national verdict is still open.

A Dallas Fed analysis finds a relative decline in Texas online postings for jobs with more generative-AI-automatable tasks. It is the clearest new regional hiring signal in the map. It does not turn exposure into proof of layoffs or make a Texas result a national verdict.

What changed

Within industries, postings for more-exposed positions were about 8% lower relative to less-exposed positions by 2025 Q1. In a balanced panel, incumbent firms with more pre-ChatGPT exposure posted about 8–9% fewer openings by early 2026. The authors calculate a 2.6% effect on total Texas Lightcast postings in 2025.

Why this is more than a forecast

The analysis uses observed online ads, not a modelled job-loss scenario. Its task score is built from Claude-use data. Yet it still measures exposure, not a verified employer rollout, and it tracks postings rather than people hired or kept in work.

Why the U.S. story did not suddenly become simple

A broad U.S. posting analysis previously found little indication of a distinct AI-driven decline. The Texas and national results differ in geography, exposure metric, window and model. They can point to a regional effect, a method difference or both. Neither source proves the answer for every worker.

What would settle more of this

Use a common method across regions, show pre-trends, observe actual employer adoption and follow openings through to hires, employment and pay. Until then, “where?” is as necessary as “how much?” in an AI jobs claim.
