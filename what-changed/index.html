Evidence update · 14 September 2026

A second early-career warning meets a broad-market brake

A new Census study finds lower initial employment and pay for graduates from the most AI-exposed U.S. majors after ChatGPT's release. That strengthens the entry-level warning. A separate Federal Reserve analysis of broad job postings does not find a distinct AI-driven drop. The gap between those findings is now the live question.

What changed

The early-career evidence map now includes a second U.S. administrative-record study. Its top AI-exposure decile had a five-percentage-point decline in initial employment and a 13% decline in full-quarter initial earnings relative to the bottom six deciles. The authors find that roughly half of the earnings gap reflects a shift into lower-paying sectors.

What the paper cannot settle

The major score is a pre-ChatGPT exposure measure, not a record of whether a graduate's employer adopted an AI system. The design needs similar post-2022 trends without ChatGPT across exposure groups. The authors test work-from-home, graduate supply and other explanations, but do not claim to rule out every field-specific shock.

The result that stops the easy story

Federal Reserve researchers find little indication that postings for AI-exposed occupations fell disproportionately after ChatGPT. Their measure is broad and studies listings, not actual hires or recent graduates. It does not cancel the graduate finding. It means the evidence does not support calling the entry signal an economy-wide collapse.

Why it matters

Entry-level damage can look different from a mature labor market. A graduate may still get work but start in a lower-paying sector, while broad vacancy data stay steady. That possibility is consequential enough to track, but it is not a personal forecast.

The next test

We need later cohorts, observed employer adoption and designs that can separate AI from simultaneous shocks to exposed fields. We also need evidence that explains the difference between graduate outcomes, early-career hires and economy-wide postings.
