AI job-risk watch · updated 30 September 2026

The AI exposure score can change with the platform behind it

A U.S. employment estimate changed sign when researchers changed only the platform usage data used to rank occupations. The study tests a measurement problem. It does not show that AI created or destroyed jobs.

What changed in the evidence map

Yin and Ogut held a U.S. employment analysis fixed but swapped the AI-platform conversation shares inside an exposure score. Consumer and enterprise channels within one vendor gave opposite signs. Workforce reweighting reduced tested coefficient magnitudes by 42–93%. These are changes in estimates, not in employment. Read the research update.

The job question is still open

Separate studies report weaker entry-level hiring in some exposed U.S. groups, mixed posted and incumbent pay, and no clear economy-wide U.S. AI jobs footprint. They do not share one exposure measure, population or outcome. The platform test does not cancel those findings, but it makes a single exposure ranking a weaker basis for a personal decision.

Check a headline before you act

  1. Does “exposed” mean AI capability, worker-reported use, employer adoption or one platform's users?
  2. Was hiring, employment, pay or only a modelled score measured?
  3. Would the result survive a different exposure measure and apply to your place and work?

At your employer, ask what changed in tasks, training access, hiring and staffing. A population study cannot predict your own job risk.
