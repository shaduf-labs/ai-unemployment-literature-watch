AI job-risk watch · updated 20 September 2026

A 4% AI jobs figure is a forecast. The hiring warning is still narrower.

A new CEPR paper puts cognitive employment 4% lower by 2030 in one “substantial change” scenario. That is a conditional model result—not a count of jobs already lost, a probability forecast or a verdict on your job. The sharpest observed warning still comes from Danish firms that reported AI adoption: smaller adopters had slower employment growth, driven by lower recruitment.

The 4% number lives inside a scenario

Korinek and colleagues model three paths for AI between 2026 and 2030: modest, substantial and extreme change. The paper says the median U.S. adult’s survey answers are consistent with its substantial path, where cognitive employment declines 4% by 2030.

That is a way to inspect an assumed future, not an observation from payroll, hiring records or unemployment claims. A model number needs its horizon and assumptions attached before it can change a career decision.

The observed warning is in recruitment

Bonin, Darougheh and Kuchler match Danish firms’ reports of AI use to monthly employer–employee records. Firms first reporting adoption in 2023 had employment growth 11% below non-adopters by late 2025, relative to their earlier trend. The result was concentrated in smaller firms and came through lower recruitment.

That is closer to an observed labor-market outcome than a scenario. It still cannot make adoption random, prove that AI alone caused the gap, or tell a worker outside Denmark what will happen next.

A future model and a hiring record are different rulers

The headline to resist

“AI will cut 4% of jobs” silently turns a model’s conditional cognitive-employment scenario into a present-tense fact. The current evidence does not support that move. It supports a more urgent but narrower conclusion: hiring may tighten in particular firms, places and entry routes before a broad unemployment signal appears—and the cause is still contested.
