AI job-risk watch · updated 17 September 2026

Texas just added a real AI hiring warning. It is not a national layoff count.

A Dallas Fed analysis finds that Texas online job postings fell more in occupations with more tasks that generative AI can automate. A broader U.S. posting analysis still finds little distinct AI-driven decline. The risk signal is now harder to dismiss—and harder to generalize.

The Texas signal is concrete

Using millions of Lightcast online ads, the Dallas Fed finds that postings for more-exposed positions were about 8% lower relative to less-exposed positions by 2025 Q1. Its aggregate calculation implies 2.6% fewer Texas Lightcast postings in 2025 because of generative-AI automation exposure. A balanced panel of existing firms shows a similar 8–9% pullback by early 2026.

This is a hiring-demand warning, especially for people trying to enter or switch fields. It is not proof that Texas workers were laid off, or that AI caused every missing ad.

What the study actually measured

The U.S. evidence is now split even on postings

A prior broad U.S. posting analysis found little sign of a distinct AI-related decline. That does not cancel Texas. The sources use different places, time windows, exposure measures and models. The honest answer is not “AI has not touched hiring” or “AI has eliminated jobs everywhere.” It is: a local pullback has appeared, and we do not yet know how far it reaches.

The danger line moved. It did not become a personal forecast.

Three U.S. studies still find weaker early-career outcomes in more exposed work. Norway does not show a robust young-worker employment drop. Now Texas adds an exposure-linked posting result. A reader needs the location, outcome, AI measure and comparison before letting any one of these findings change a career plan.
