AI job-risk watch · updated 6 October 2026

A factory AI pay gap is not a raise for workers

Federal Reserve research finds that U.S. manufacturing ads asking for AI skills advertised about 70% higher average pay than manufacturing ads overall. It did not track a worker getting that pay, a hire or a layoff.

What changed

Broad AI skills appeared in about 11% of manufacturing job ads in late 2025, compared with 8% across sectors. Explicit generative-AI skills remained in under 1% of manufacturing ads through July 2026. The 70% pay gap compares different ads and is not adjusted for occupation, employer or location. A production-occupation subset showed a roughly 30% average posted gap since 2023; that is not a pay rise for current production workers.

The job-loss claim is still narrower

Separate U.S. posting research finds that listed tasks changed within comparable job categories, without showing that AI caused a worker's job to change. Junior-entry studies warn of weaker hiring in specific samples. A broad U.S. tracker has not found a clear economy-wide AI jobs footprint. None gives your personal odds.

Check the job claim

  1. Does “AI skill” mean machine learning, generative AI, or either?
  2. Is the pay number from an ad, an accepted offer or a worker's wages?
  3. Are different roles, employers and places being compared?

These distinctions matter before spending money on training. The research does not prescribe a course or predict your job.
