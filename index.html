AI job-risk watch · checked 19 October 2026

6.1 million workers could struggle to recover if AI displaces them

A U.S. study places 6.1 million workers in occupations with both high AI task exposure and low average capacity to manage a job transition. It does not count 6.1 million lost jobs. The danger is conditional: if displacement happens, savings, transferable skills and local vacancies affect the room to respond.

Latest check: no change to the jobs answer

A Swedish matched-hire study found an AI-skill pay gap of about 5–6% versus occupational peers that vanished within the same firm. It studied skills to develop or implement AI, not ordinary chatbot use. It does not show that training raises a worker’s wage.

An Argentine randomized task experiment found that AI access narrowed an education-based performance gap. It did not measure job offers or pay. A stronger task result does not prove that a worker can reach a better job.

A U.S. worker survey found generative AI use across many occupations, but most measured tasks still had less than half of workers using it. It counted reported use, not job losses, wages or hours.

A U.S. STEM PhD study links detected AI writing in dissertations to different academic and industry career paths. It does not show that writing with AI caused the difference or cost jobs.

A U.S. sector study finds about 4% higher relative employment by 2024 where AI could assist tasks. It does not observe employer AI use. Its automatable-task result is statistically inconclusive, not proof that no jobs were cut. The study adds a different denominator, not an all-clear.

Why the risk remains open

Two Copilot studies point in different directions on technical hiring but count different outcomes in different firms. Broad U.S. tracking has not found a clear aggregate AI job-loss footprint in its measured window; that does not erase narrow risks. A sector can grow while its entry-level route narrows.

Before acting on a risk headline

  1. Does it count hires, employment, layoffs or only tasks that AI might affect?
  2. Does it observe employer AI use or assign a task-exposure score?
  3. Does it cover your occupation, location and the period you care about?

No study here calculates your personal odds or proves that buying training will protect your job.
