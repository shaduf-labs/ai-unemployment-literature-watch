AI job-risk watch · updated 25 September 2026

AI-exposed jobs are posting higher pay. The entry-level door may still be closing.

A new U.S. job-posting analysis finds advertised pay rose faster in highly AI-exposed occupations while entry-level ads fell from 29% to 10% of their postings. That is a warning about the shape of opportunity, not proof that AI raised pay, cut junior jobs or changed your odds.

What the new analysis measured

Indeed Hiring Lab analyzed millions of U.S. job postings that included an advertised salary. It grouped occupations by a score for the share of job skills that generative AI could transform, then compared advertised pay before and after ChatGPT’s release.

Pay in the most-exposed group rose about 46% from 2021 to mid-2026, compared with 25% in the least-exposed group. The post-ChatGPT exposure-linked premium is 5.7% in the occupation-level model and 4.7% when matching job titles to their own past. The analysis is about ads, not paychecks or hiring records.

The pressure point is the first rung

In the most-exposed occupations, entry-level ads fell from 29% to 10% of postings between 2021 and 2026. Senior ads rose from 22% to 47%. A higher advertised wage can therefore sit beside fewer early-career chances to get in.

That pattern matches the concern raised by Texas and Census graduate records, but it does not prove the same cause. Indeed uses exposure, not employer AI deployment. Hiring cycles, industry mix and a shift to senior roles can all affect the result.

Why the pay headline does not settle the risk

When the analysis holds seniority mix constant, the exposure-linked premium narrows to 2.4% and is not statistically significant. Advertised salaries are also not realized wages. The honest conclusion is narrower: the market may be rewarding experienced work in exposed fields while making the entry route thinner. The evidence does not yet show that AI caused that change.

The claim check that matters now

Headline saysAsk first
“AI workers are earning more.”Does the source measure posted pay or workers’ realized earnings? Did seniority or occupation mix change?
“AI killed entry-level jobs.”Did the study count junior ads, hires, employment or an exposure score? Did it observe AI adoption?
“There is no AI jobs effect.”Does the evidence cover entry routes, job quality, a region and early-adopting firms—or only a broad average?

What would change this answer

Watch for studies that link employer AI rollout to posted pay, offered pay, accepted wages, hiring and seniority within the same firms. A repeated result after those comparisons would strengthen the causal case. A result that vanishes after accounting for seniority and non-AI changes would weaken it.

Before this changes your plans

  • Updates — see how posted pay, graduate earnings and unemployment tell different stories.
  • Study library — inspect the pay and seniority result beside administrative records and broad monitoring.
  • Read claims — separate exposure, adoption, job ads, wages and employment.

Sources: Indeed Hiring Lab; Federal Reserve Bank of Dallas; U.S. Census Bureau; Yale Budget Lab.
