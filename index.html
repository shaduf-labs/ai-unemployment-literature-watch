AI job-risk watch · updated 21 September 2026

A real AI grant raised job ads. It did not show a jobs boom or a jobs collapse.

A Swedish study compared firms that won first-time AI-project grants with firms that applied but were not funded. Five years later, awardees were more likely to post vacancies, especially white-collar vacancies. The study did not detect a change in total employment, hiring or separations. That is a serious check on both AI layoff claims and “AI creates jobs” claims.

What the Swedish test can show

Hellsten, Khanna, Lodefalk and Yakymovych use a synthetic difference-in-differences design on 190 Swedish small- and mid-sized firms that applied for Vinnova AI-project support in 2019–2020. Fifty-three were funded; 137 were not. Five years after the award, funded firms were 24 percentage points more likely to post a vacancy and 22.5 points more likely to post a white-collar vacancy.

The paper did not find a statistically detectable change in total employment, hires or separations. A vacancy is an attempt to recruit. It is not a filled job, and it is not a layoff count.

Why this is not your job forecast

The treatment was a Swedish grant for a first-time AI project, not an experiment with one named AI system. The period mostly predates widespread generative-AI chatbot use. The result therefore identifies a grant effect among Swedish applicant firms, not a universal effect of generative AI on every worker or country.

The study also cannot say why a vacancy was created, whether it was filled, or whether a different labor market would respond the same way. Sweden’s labor shortages and institutions matter to the result.

Three studies, three different rulers

EvidenceWhat it measuresWhat it cannot settle
Swedish AI-grant studyThe effect of grant award on vacancy posting, hiring, separations and total employment among applicant firms.A universal effect of generative AI, or whether vacancies became jobs.
Danish reported-adoption studyEmployment growth and recruitment at firms that reported AI use, compared with non-adopters.Whether AI alone caused slower recruitment, or whether it applies outside Denmark.
U.S. early-career and posting studiesHiring, initial employment, pay, payroll employment, vacancies or unemployment under exposure-based comparisons.A common adoption-based explanation across every outcome, country or age group.

The 4% number is still a scenario

A CEPR paper’s 4% decline in cognitive employment belongs to one 2030 scenario. It is neither a count of jobs already gone nor a probability forecast. The Swedish evidence is more concrete, but it still shows a grant effect on vacancies—not a personal job forecast.

Before this changes your plans

  • Updates — why more job ads did not become a detected employment effect.
  • Study library — inspect the grant study beside hiring, unemployment and scenario evidence.
  • Read claims — separate an exposure score, grant, adoption measure, vacancy, hire and headcount claim.

Sources: Hellsten, Khanna, Lodefalk and Yakymovych, IZA Discussion Paper 18267; Bonin, Darougheh and Kuchler, Danmarks Nationalbank; Korinek and colleagues, CEPR.
