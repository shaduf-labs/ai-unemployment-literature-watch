AI job-risk watch · updated 29 September 2026

AI-written pitches can blur experience. That is not a jobs verdict.

A controlled online experiment found that ChatGPT-assisted pitches made prior expertise harder to judge on average. It did not measure whether anyone lost a job or an offer. In one non-English-speaking group, AI assistance improved screening accuracy.

What the new study measured

Writers prepared hiring or startup pitches with and without ChatGPT assistance. Evaluators then guessed whether the writers had relevant prior experience. Across 6,440 pooled evaluations, accuracy fell by 4–9% when assisted text was available. This was a simulated task, not a real vacancy. A separate field-experiment abstract reports better cover letters but no gain in its interview-invitation outcome. Read the full research update.

Where the hiring risk begins

If written applications are less informative, an employer may seek another signal. The experiment measured evaluator accuracy; it did not show which screening rule employers chose or how many people were hired. A recent model predicts a possible barrier for inexperienced applicants, but that market effect remains untested.

Other evidence stays separate. U.S. occupation data show higher posted pay but lower pay for workers who stayed in more LLM-exposed work. U.S. entry studies report narrow warnings, while a broad monitor has not found a clear economy-wide AI jobs footprint.

Before you act on an AI hiring headline

  1. Did the study observe a written application, an interview, an offer, a hire or employment?
  2. Were applicants and employers in a real market, or in a controlled exercise?
  3. Did the result differ by language background, experience or job type?

These checks narrow a claim. They cannot predict your personal job prospects.
