AI job-risk watch · updated 11 October 2026

Copilot may slow technical hiring. Another study finds more hiring months.

One research comparison finds two different signals. A conference abstract estimates about 35% fewer technical hires in selected large Copilot adopters; a separate journal abstract links adoption to a 3–5% higher monthly chance of hiring a software engineer. A hiring count and the chance of any hire are not the same outcome.

What changed

The negative estimate uses software-contract renewal timing to study adoption among 5,425 multinationals. It reports no rise in separations. The positive association uses LinkedIn and GitHub records and says entry-level hiring drove the higher firm-month hiring probability. Neither full paper was accessible in this run, so these figures are provisional in this watch.

What remains unresolved

The studies differ in firm sample, comparison and hiring denominator. They cannot be averaged into a net jobs number, nor used to infer your personal odds. The junior software vacancy decline is a third measure: ads, not hires. Broad U.S. labor data still do not show a clear economy-wide AI job-loss footprint.

Before acting on a hiring headline

  1. Does it count total hires, the chance of any hire, vacancies or job separations?
  2. Was employer AI use observed, inferred from a contract, or scored as exposure?
  3. Does the study cover your place, occupation and career stage?

None of these studies shows that buying a course or leaving a job improves an individual's outcome.
