AI job-risk watch · updated 16 September 2026

The early-career AI warning just hit a hard Nordic counter-signal.

Three U.S. studies find worse early-career outcomes in more AI-exposed work. A new Norwegian population study does not find a robust young-worker employment drop. That is not an all-clear or a reversal. It means the first alarming pattern has not yet survived a country, method and pre-trend test.

What the evidence says now

FindingWhat was measuredWhat it cannot prove
U.S. entry routes look weaker in highly exposed work.Payroll, Census hiring and graduate records compare young workers, industry-state cells or majors with different occupational AI-exposure scores.That a firm’s generative-AI rollout caused every gap. Exposure, remote work, education and other post-2022 changes still move together.
Norway does not show a robust matching drop.Population-wide Norwegian employer-employee records through March 2025. Three designs test employment for workers aged 22–25 in highly exposed occupations.That the U.S. results are wrong, or that Norway proves AI is safe. The study uses exposure, not observed adoption, and Norway is not the U.S. labor market.
Actual adopters do not all look like cutters.A selected U.S. firm-spending study reports headcount growth among heavy AI spenders; a broader EIB AI-adoption model reports no adverse firm-level employment effect.That adoption creates jobs. The measures, firms, time periods and outcomes differ, and neither result maps a reader’s own job.
Broad U.S. postings still lack a distinct AI drop.A Federal Reserve analysis tests whether postings in AI-exposed occupations fell disproportionately after ChatGPT.What happened to actual hires, graduates or entry routes that postings do not capture.

The danger line moved. It did not disappear.

The U.S. entry signal is serious enough to watch. But “young people in exposed work are being pushed out by AI” is now too broad. Norway found negative estimates, yet they were not robust, and pre-2022 trends could create part of the apparent break.

The useful pressure point is narrower: are employers that actually deploy AI changing entry hiring, pay or job design after comparable pre-trends? We still do not have a decisive answer.

A country is part of the method

Norway’s registers cover a different labor market with different institutions, firms and adoption patterns. That makes it a needed counter-signal, not a universal verdict. A U.S. payroll gap, a Norwegian null and a global exposure score cannot be averaged into your personal odds.
